## 2. Headline

The Latest Oil Loans Could Push Our Reserve Below the Floor

## 3. Body, part one

```
The Energy Department has lent out another 40.55 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, or SPR. If the companies take all of it, and no borrowed oil comes back first, the reserve would hold about 242 million barrels. That is below the 250 million to 300 million barrels that some experts call the reserve's practical bottom, and it would be the lowest level since early 1982.

So we may soon find out whether those experts are right.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
The two rounds went to different kinds of borrowers. In the first, a refiner, ExxonMobil, took 67.2% of the oil. In the second, trading companies took 91.9%: Trafigura, Gunvor, Vitol and Macquarie. These are firms that buy and sell oil but do not run U.S. refineries.

These are still loans. The oil is due back between April 2027 and December 2029, plus extra barrels. But the last time the government lent oil, in late 2021 and early 2022, return dates moved back on 10 of 13 loans. In short, it's best to take these payback dates with a grain of salt.

So how low can the reserve go? The Energy Department has said at least 70 million barrels must stay in it. A Texas A&M petroleum engineering professor put the practical floor at 250 million to 300 million. Analysts at Rapidan Energy describe a floor of about 170 million. Congress wrote 252.4 million into law, but only for one kind of short emergency drawdown.

In the post, I walk through who borrowed the oil, what the first round could pay, and where each expert draws the line. The weekly numbers this winter will show how low the reserve goes. Click the button below to follow along.
```
